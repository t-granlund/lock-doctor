03 · Where the friction lives

Small stuff. Fixable stuff.

Said with respect — these are the same frictions every 5–10 person trade shop carries. Each one was verified today, and each one is something an apprentice that lives on your machine can absorb.

The parked domain verified

lockdoctor.co sits parked at a registrar while the real site is lockdoctorar.com. Old links, old cards, old word-of-mouth spelling "lockdoctor" — they die at a GoDaddy parking page. Brand split costs type-ins and trust.

Hours disagree across the internet verified

Your site footer says Mon–Sat 6 AM–10 PM, closed Sunday. Bing tells people you close at midnight. Google may say something else. Search engines penalize inconsistent listings, and a customer who drives to a closed shop doesn't leave five stars.

No published pricing — anywhere verified

Every call starts with "what would you charge?" — which means your phone is the pricing page. One competitor (a national franchise) publishes $150–300 anchors and lets the phone ring less. Ranges, not quotes, would filter tire-kickers and convert ready buyers.

The phone is the whole front door verified

No BBB profile, no online booking, no after-hours intake. Every lead arrives live or not at all — 10 PM to 6 AM belongs to whoever answers. That's the solo-founder tax, and you have a crew to spread it on.

Reviews shown are curated verified

The site's review widget shows five-star quotes only, with no link to the live aggregate. Shoppers notice. Linking out to the raw Google listing converts better than a highlight reel — real includes the occasional 3-star, and that's what makes the 5-stars believable.

The knowledge lives in heads pattern

22 years of vehicle coverage, fob part numbers, "which van has which machine" — if it isn't written down, it can't be delegated, and Greg's phone stays the bottleneck. (Ask us how we know the fob part numbers matter: the difference between a "rare" 315 MHz fob and a common 902 MHz one is literally a note in a file.)

04 · What the grove would build

An apprentice on your own machine

Spruce Grove OS is an agentic coding harness that runs locally — it doesn't sell you software, it builds the small tools your shop actually needs, then keeps maintaining them. Not mockups; this pattern is live today: a lead-intake system (leads.bentondrones.com: form, geocode, ticket, dashboard) and a full booking site for Bentonville Barber Company, both built by the agent and shipped to real doors.

Listing consistency audit

Agent crawls Google, Bing, Yelp, Facebook for Lock Doctor; reports every hours/phone/service mismatch; drafts the exact corrections. You approve, it logs. Fixes opportunity #2 permanently.

A pricing page that filters

Publish ranges for the ten most common jobs. Agent drafts, you set the numbers, it deploys. The phone keeps ringing — for booking, not for interrogation.

After-hours intake

Voicemail-to-text lands in a folder; every morning the agent hands each tech a callback list with name, address, vehicle, and a drafted first response. Nights stop being lost leads.

The parts brain

A local SQLite of fob part numbers, FCC IDs, what's in which van, what's low. "2022 Bronco Sport" returns the right 902 MHz part instantly — the difference between a pass and a booking.

Review follow-through

After each job, the agent drafts a thank-you + review link text, ready to send, and flags every new public review so nothing sits unanswered. You keep the send button.

The Friday one-pager

Jobs, revenue by service, reviews, no-shows, low stock — one page, every Friday, from CSVs you already have. Five minutes of reading replaces an hour of receipts.

"Remove technology obstacles" isn't a slogan here — it's the mission statement of the whole grove. The apprentice handles the screen; Greg and the crew stay present with the people in the driveway.

06 · Why this is safe

The harness is the key, not the models

This is the part most vendors get backwards, so it gets its own section.

Models are rented. The harness is owned.

Any AI model — OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Bedrock, Z.AI, or a private endpoint — is a commodity you swap by changing one line of config. What makes an agent trustworthy isn't which brain it borrows; it's the harness: the permission gates that decide what commands may run, the approval step before anything consequential, the skills and memory that live in files you own. That asset sits on your machine, not in someone's cloud.

Local-first means a smaller breach surface

Customer names, addresses, job history — in a typical SaaS stack those live in six vendor databases you don't control. In the grove lane they live in local files and SQLite on your own hardware, encrypted at rest, backed up where you say. There is no third-party pile of your customers to breach.

Sovereignty is a practiced discipline

Spruce Grove OS ships with a sovereignty playbook: MIT license, an offline reinstall wheel, your own git mirrors. If every vendor and every model provider vanished tonight, nothing breaks — the apprentice keeps working, because the whole stack is yours. No vendor can raise prices and hold your workflow hostage. SaaS does that every day.

Honest limits, stated plainly

Model calls send prompt text to the model provider over encrypted connections — so the rules are: API keys stay in local config, never in prompts; customer PII stays out of prompts by default; anything consequential waits for a human yes. The harness makes those rules easy to follow. It can't make carelessness impossible — nothing can, and anything that claims to is selling you something.
